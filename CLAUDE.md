- `npm run smoke` — dependency-free smoke test (`scripts/smoke.mjs`) against a running server, `BASE_URL` env (default `http://localhost:4321`). Every refusal by the database asserts its cause (status, `code` and the message that names the table or function), not a status class. The absences are covered the same way: the `absences` table through PostgREST (RLS, the column grants, the constraints, the cascade from the employee), the `/api/absences` routes, the planner's `/absences` pages (the default and the full view of the list with their notes and links, the days with their number, the type hint, the status of the edit page) and the absence scenarios of the assignment page (collisions and their weights, nights, the corrected norm, the neighbour steps). It also covers the invitation flow: the `user_id` / `invited_at` / `activated_at` columns, the own-row policy and the `mark_account_active` function, the mail templates, `POST /api/employees/<id>/invite` (invite, invite again, password reset, a foreign account), the `/auth/set-password` page and route with tokens taken from the admin API (`generate_link`; it never reads mails), and what the planner's pages show (account column, invite button, locked e-mail field). It creates planner, employee and role-less accounts through the admin API of a local Supabase, so it needs `SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_KEY` and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` (values from `supabase status -o env`; the app under test must read the same stack, with the same service-role key in its `.dev.vars`, because the invitation route reads it there), exits before any request unless the `SUPABASE_URL` host is `127.0.0.1` or `localhost`, and must never be run against a hosted project or the production Worker. Run after dependency upgrades; CI runs it against the production preview with a local Supabase (its Mailpit container stays up, because Auth needs a mail server to hand an invitation to).
  - The assignment page is also checked against the live rules on real data, on a schedule of its own (`Smoke-<runId>-rules`: the week 02.11 to 08.11.2026, which has no holiday, so the norm of a full-time employee is 40 h), with four employees of a full FTE, one for each scenario, and three shifts (the two shifts of the API steps, 06:00-14:00 and 22:00-07:00, and a 01:00-09:00 shift that the steps create), put on the cells through `POST /api/schedules/<id>/assignments`. The steps read the HTML of `/schedules/<id>/assign` and assert the counter, the exact ordered list (the weight and the whole reason, built from the data of the step, so a wrong person, day or number fails), the column "Wskazania" of each person and the marks of the day cells: a shift of 9 h alone (more than 8 h in the day, with the art. 135 sentence), a break of 8 h between two shifts of a day (the daily rest and the hours of the day that starts with the first shift), a break of exactly 11 h (allowed, so no daily-rest item, while the 24 hours from the first shift still hold 13 h), seven days in a row (56 h in the week, no weekly rest of 35 h and over the norm) and the shortages (a warning for each cell with fewer people than it needs; a cell in excess or complete gives none). The neighbour steps then take one assignment off through `DELETE` at a time and read the page again (and the table after each `DELETE`): only what that assignment caused goes, the other person on the same shift and day and the same person on another day keep their items, 48 h in the week is the limit itself and allowed, and the free time at both edges of the schedule counts as one rest. The coverage table, the pressed buttons, the fixed note on the edges and the absence of `role="alert"` are asserted in the same page reads.
  - The hours against the norm (S-06, the column "Godziny" of the people's table) are checked on a schedule of their own (`Smoke-<runId>-hours`: the week from Tuesday 10.11.2026, whose Wednesday 11.11 is a holiday, so the norm of a full-time employee is 32 h and that of a half-time one 16 h), with seven employees (one on 1/2 FTE) and the two shifts of the API steps (06:00-14:00 and 22:00-07:00), put on the cells through `POST /api/schedules/<id>/assignments`. The steps assert the exact text of the column of each person ("8 h z 32 h mniej o 24 h"; two shifts on different days "17 h z 32 h mniej o 15 h"; a night shift on the last day, counted in full, "9 h z 32 h mniej o 23 h"; nobody "0 min z 32 h mniej o 32 h"; a half-time employee "8 h z 16 h mniej o 8 h"; exactly the norm "32 h z 32 h równo z normą"; above it "40 h z 32 h więcej o 8 h"), the header "Godziny" after "Wskazania", the paragraph under the table, and that the column has no `role`, no `aria-live`, no icon and no colour class of a status. The neighbour steps take one assignment off through `DELETE` at a time and read the page again: only the text of that person changes. The other assignment schedules (the API, route and rules schedules) have their column asserted in the page steps that already read their rows, the rules schedule in each of its four states.

## Architecture

**Astro 7 SSR app** with React 19 islands, Tailwind 4, Supabase auth, and shadcn/ui components. Deployed to Cloudflare Workers.

### Rendering mode

Full server-side rendering (`output: "server"` in astro.config.mjs). All pages are server-rendered by default. API routes must export `const prerender = false`.

### Auth flow

- `src/lib/supabase.ts` — creates a Supabase SSR client using `@supabase/ssr` with cookie-based sessions. Uses `astro:env/server` for `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_KEY` (server-only secrets declared in astro.config.mjs `env.schema`).
- `src/middleware.ts` — runs on every request, resolves the current user and role (`app_metadata.role`, never `user_metadata`), attaches them to `context.locals.user` / `context.locals.role`, and gates the request with `decideAccess` from `src/lib/services/access.ts`. Default deny: a path missing from the table in `access.ts` is planner-only. A denial is 401 (anonymous) or 403 (wrong role): JSON under `/api/`, otherwise the `/access-denied` page via `next("/access-denied")` (not `context.rewrite()`).
- API endpoints: `src/pages/api/auth/{signin,signout,set-password}.ts`
- Auth pages: `src/pages/auth/signin.astro` and `src/pages/auth/set-password.astro` (public: the link in an invitation or password-reset mail opens it, and the token in the link is the credential)
- Protected page example: `src/pages/dashboard.astro`
- Public registration is closed (no sign-up page or endpoint, `[auth] enable_signup = false` in `supabase/config.toml`); accounts are created through the Supabase admin API: employee accounts by the invitation route (below), the first planner by a script. `node scripts/create-planner.mjs <email>` creates the first planner or promotes an existing account (sets `app_metadata.role` to `planner`). It needs `SUPABASE_URL` and `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`, plus `PLANNER_PASSWORD` only for a new account, and works against hosted projects. For this script the service-role key goes into the environment of that one command.
- **Service-role key** (`SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`, bypasses RLS): a Worker secret in production (`npx wrangler secret put SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`) and, for local dev and the production preview, an entry in the gitignored `.dev.vars` that holds the key of the local stack only (never a hosted project's key). `astro.config.mjs` declares it as an optional server secret and only `src/lib/supabase-admin.ts` reads it. Only the invitation route `src/pages/api/employees/[id]/invite.ts` may import that module (ESLint `no-restricted-imports` enforces it): do not use the key anywhere else, scripts under `scripts/` excepted, and do not read data with it. The route reads the employee with the planner's session (RLS), and the admin client only talks to Auth and writes `employees.user_id` and `invited_at`.
- **Invitation flow**: `POST /api/employees/[id]/invite` (planner-only, no body) invites a new employee (`inviteUserByEmail`, then `app_metadata.role = "employee"`, then `user_id` and `invited_at`; a failure after the mail removes the account again), sends a new link to an account that never confirmed, and sends a password-reset link to an active one; an address that has a foreign confirmed account gets 409 `account_exists`, and a linked account whose address is not the employee's gets 409 `account_mismatch`. The database refuses a new e-mail for an employee with an account (trigger `employees_keep_account_email`, `PT409`), the link is written only while the row still has the invited address and the previous `user_id`, and the compensation removes only an account made by that very request that no employee holds (`isFreshInvitedAccount`, `mayBecomeEmployee`). A mail limit answers 429 `rate_limited`, and a mail that went out with a failed `invited_at` write 500 `sent_not_recorded`. The decision is the pure `decideInviteAction` in `src/lib/services/invitation.ts`. The mails are the templates `supabase/templates/{invite,recovery}.html`, configured in `supabase/config.toml` together with `otp_expiry = 86400` (a link works for 24 hours, once; the longest life the hosted project accepts) and `minimum_password_length = 8`; their links open `/auth/set-password?token_hash=...&type=invite|recovery`. Opening the page spends nothing (mail scanners open links): `POST /api/auth/set-password` (JSON `{ tokenHash, type, password }`) checks the password with `validatePassword` (`src/lib/services/password.ts`, the rule of the PRD: at least 8 characters, an upper-case letter, a digit and a special character) before it spends the token with `verifyOtp` and sets the password with `updateUser`, which also signs the employee in. The form is the `src/components/auth/SetPasswordForm.tsx` island with the `useSetPasswordForm` hook. The password rule is enforced in the app only, not by Supabase. Once the password is saved, the same route calls `supabase.rpc("mark_account_active")` with the employee's own session, also for `recovery` links; a failure of that call is only logged, the answer stays 200.

### Data access

- Read and write data through `Astro.locals.supabase` (the user's session, set per request by the middleware), so RLS decides what a user sees and may change. Query functions live in `src/lib/` (`src/lib/employees.ts`, `src/lib/shifts.ts`), never in pages or routes.
- Validate input with one function shared by the form and the API route, so both give the same answer (`validateEmployeeInput` in `src/lib/services/employee.ts`, built on zod; `validateShiftInput` in `src/lib/services/shift.ts`, plain checks); the server validates again whatever the form sent.
- Employee pages are planner-only: `src/pages/employees/` inside `src/layouts/PlannerLayout.astro`, with the `src/components/employees/EmployeeForm.tsx` island and its `useEmployeeForm` hook (`src/components/hooks/`), saving through `/api/employees` and `/api/employees/[id]`.
- Employee accounts: `employees.user_id` (unique, links the row to its Auth account) and `employees.invited_at` are written only with the service-role key by the invitation route; the column grants let a session write the five roster columns and nothing else. `employees.activated_at` (when the employee first set a password) is written only by the SQL function `public.mark_account_active()` (`security definer`, `set search_path = ''`, execute for signed-in callers only), which `POST /api/auth/set-password` calls with the employee's session: it sets the column on the caller's own row, only for the `employee` role and only while it is null, so the first activation wins and a password reset keeps it. This was the first `security definer` function of the project (the second is `approve_schedule`, see the approval paragraphs below) (a deliberate departure from the first plan of S-10, approved by the author on 2026-11-23); the admin client does not write the column. An employee reads only their own row (policy `employee can select own employee row`). Once `user_id` is set the e-mail cannot change (`emailChangeBlocked` and `EMAIL_LOCKED_MESSAGE` in `src/lib/services/invitation.ts`; the API answers 422 and the form field is read-only). The employee list and the edit page show the account state (`inviteStatus`, `formatInviteLabel`: "Bez konta", "Zaproszony ...", or "Zarejestrowany dd.mm.rrrr" once `activated_at` is set) and the `src/components/employees/InviteButton.tsx` island with its `useInviteEmployee` hook, which calls the invitation route (the button reads "Zaproś", "Wyślij ponownie" or, for an active account, "Resetuj hasło").
- Shift pages (the shift dictionary: name, start and end time) follow the same pattern and are planner-only too: `src/pages/shifts/` inside `PlannerLayout`, with the `src/components/shifts/ShiftForm.tsx` island and its `useShiftForm` hook (`src/components/hooks/`), saving through `/api/shifts` and `/api/shifts/[id]`. The shared PostgREST helpers (`isUuid`, `logDatabaseError`) live in `src/lib/postgrest.ts`.
- Schedule pages (the weekly schedule draft: a start date, an optional label, a staffing grid of shifts x 7 days with the required headcount, and the employees it covers) are planner-only as well: `src/pages/schedules/` inside `PlannerLayout`, with the D6 notice (`src/components/schedules/ScheduleNotice.astro`: a draft does not replace the formal work-time schedule), the `src/components/schedules/ScheduleForm.tsx` island (its `StaffingGrid.tsx` and `EmployeePicker.tsx`) and its `useScheduleForm` hook (`src/components/hooks/`), saving through `/api/schedules` and `/api/schedules/[id]`. A schedule spans three tables (`schedules`, `schedule_staffing`, `schedule_employees`) and is saved in one transaction by the SQL function `save_schedule` (security invoker, called as RPC from `src/lib/schedules.ts`), so a save through the app stores everything or nothing; nobody deletes a schedule through the app. The invariants of a saved schedule (at least one cell and one employee, at most 350 cells, the shape of the JSON arguments) are kept by the app's validation, not forced by the database: a planner's token that bypasses the app can still `delete` rows of `schedule_staffing` and `schedule_employees` through PostgREST, and the cascade then removes their assignments without the confirmation that `save_schedule` asks for. The rules (dates as `YYYY-MM-DD` with UTC arithmetic, `validateScheduleInput`, sorting) are pure functions in `src/lib/services/schedule.ts`. The pages pass the island plain data (`toShiftOptions` and `toEmployeeOptions` in `src/lib/schedule-options.ts`), never an employee's e-mail address, and are sent with `Cache-Control: no-store`. The list at `/schedules` reads the view `schedule_summaries` (migration `*_schedule_summaries.sql`, `security_invoker` so the planner-only RLS of its tables decides, `select` for signed-in users only) with the number of people and the total required headcount counted in the database, newest first, at most `SCHEDULE_LIST_LIMIT` (100) rows and a note on the page when more exist. `src/lib/schedules.ts` takes its comparators (`compareText`, `compareCells`) and the mapping of database errors of a save (`classifySaveFailure`) from `src/lib/services/schedule.ts`; the invisible-character rules of labels and shift names are shared through `src/lib/services/text-chars.ts`.
- Assignments (an employee on a shift on a day of a schedule, put there by hand) are planner-only too. The table `schedule_assignments` has a `source` column, `manual` (the only value S-04 writes) or `generated` (reserved for S-09), and hangs on the staffing cell and the roster entry of its schedule through composite foreign keys with `on delete cascade`, so the database itself refuses an assignment in a cell without staffing or for a person outside the schedule; it has no update (an entry is identified by its key: correcting one means taking it off and adding another). The data module is `src/lib/assignments.ts`, the rules (`validateAssignmentInput`, `groupByEmployeeDay`, `summarizeCoverage`) are pure functions in `src/lib/services/assignment.ts`. The view is `/schedules/<id>/assign` (`src/pages/schedules/[id]/assign.astro` in `PlannerLayout` with `wide`, the `src/components/schedules/AssignmentGrid.tsx` island and its `useAssignments` hook): a people x days table of shift buttons (`aria-pressed`), each click saved at once through `POST` or `DELETE` on `/api/schedules/[id]/assignments` (both idempotent), and under it the coverage of each shift and day as "x z y" with `−n` or `+n` as text, which is information and not an error: an excess is only information, a shortage is also a warning on the violations list (S-05), and the coverage table itself stays information. Since S-04 the save of a schedule is a diff: `save_schedule` (six parameters; it sets the count of a staying cell in place through the column grant `update (required_count)` on `schedule_staffing`) keeps the assignments of a cell or person that stays and, when it would delete one that has assignments, raises `PT409` (the route answers 409 `assignments_would_be_removed` with `removed`) unless the body of `PUT /api/schedules/[id]` has `confirmRemoval: true`, which the form sends once the planner has confirmed.
- Live rule violations (S-05, the check of the work-time rules while the planner assigns). Under the people's table the assignment view lists the violations of the current state of the grid, counted again in the island after every click from the optimistic `list` of `useAssignments` (so a click that is taken back after a failed save takes its violations back too; there is no request and nothing is stored, no migration and no table). The hook is `src/components/hooks/useViolations.ts` (one `useMemo` over `evaluateSchedule`), the list is `src/components/schedules/ViolationList.tsx` (critical first, each item the weight in words and the reason; the counter is the one paragraph with `aria-live="polite"`, and a change of the list is never a `role="alert"`; a fixed paragraph says the edges of the schedule are not checked and that Shiftly warns and does not guarantee legal compliance). The marks in the day cells ("Krytyczne: n", "Ostrzeżenie: n") and the column "Wskazania" of each person (a count in words, or "brak") come from `src/lib/services/violation-view.ts`. The rules are pure functions on whole minutes with no time zone in them: `src/lib/services/rules.ts` (`evaluateSchedule`, `summarizeViolations` and the rules one by one), `src/lib/services/work-hours.ts` (`shiftInterval`, `workedMinutesByEmployee`, `weeklyNorm`, `exceedsNorm`), `src/lib/services/calendar.ts` (`weekdayOf`, `polishHolidays`, `holidaysInWeek`: the holidays of 2020 to 2099 are computed, Easter included, and 24 December is a day off from 2025) and `src/lib/services/fte.ts` (`formatFteLabel`).
- The `Violation` contract in `src/types.ts` is what S-07 (absences), S-08 (approval and the saved violations) and S-09 (the generator) build on: `key` (the stable identity `<rule>|<employee id or ->|<anchor keys>`, with no number, index or time in it, so the same finding keeps its key whatever the order of the input; for `daily_hours` the key holds only the assignment that opens the 24 h window, while `anchors` lists all of them, so another shift added to the same window does not change the key), `severity` (`critical` or `warning`), `rule` (`daily_rest`, `daily_hours`, `weekly_hours`, `weekly_rest`, `absence`, `weekly_norm`, `understaffed` or `absence_requested`), `employeeId` (null for a staffing cell), `anchors` (the assignments, or with a null `employeeId` the staffing cell, the finding sits on; empty for a whole week of one person), `reason` (the Polish text with the person, the day and the numbers, which S-08 can freeze as text) and `actual` / `limit` / `unit` (`minutes` or `people`). The result is deterministic and in the canonical order: the weight (critical first), the rule (in the order above, which is `RULE_RANK` in `rules.ts`), the person (by id), then the key, all in plain code-unit order (`compareText`, never a collator).
- Decisions of the rules (S-05). The weekly norm (40 h times the FTE, less 8 h times the FTE for each holiday on a day other than Sunday, and, since S-07, less the approved absences of the person, see the absences paragraphs) is a warning and not a critical violation, because the norm of the law is monthly and averaged, so one week above it is not a breach by itself; what is critical are the limits every week must meet: 11 h of rest between two shifts, 8 h in a day, 48 h in a week and a weekly rest of 35 h (24 h when the shifts before and after the break start at different times of the day). The day is the 24 hours counted from the start of a shift (art. 128 § 3 pt 1), not the calendar day, and the hours in it are the minutes the person really works in that window; a shift longer than 8 h is critical, with the sentence that the equivalent working-time system (art. 135) is not supported. The week of the 48 h and of the weekly rest is the seven days of the schedule (a week of the calendar month almost never matches them), and the free time at the end and at the start of the schedule counts as ONE break across the edge (it needs the full 35 h, having no shift on the far side to compare, and it is the one the reason names when it ties with another break), so the edges never give a false critical violation. Only one schedule is judged: the rest between it and the previous or the next week is not checked (the page says so), a night shift from the last day counts in full, and nothing is read from other schedules. A shortage is one warning for each staffing cell with fewer people than it needs, and an excess is no violation. An assignment to a shift that is not in the dictionary has no hours, so the time rules leave it out. The thresholds are fixed, not configurable, and Shiftly warns without guaranteeing legal compliance.
- The islands now receive each employee's FTE (`EmployeeOption.fte`) and the start and end of each shift in minutes (`ShiftOption.startMinute`, `endMinute`), which the rules of the assignment view count with (they used to get the names and the formatted times only). `toShiftOptions` and `toEmployeeOptions` are shared, so besides the assignment island the islands of the schedule form pages (`/schedules/new` and `/schedules/<id>`) get them too, although only the assignment view needs them; all these pages are planner-only and sent with `Cache-Control: no-store`. An employee's e-mail address still never reaches a page: `toEmployeeOptions` copies the id, the name and the FTE only, and `src/lib/schedule-options.test.ts` pins the exact keys.
- Hours against the norm (S-06, the column "Godziny" of the assignment view). Each person's row ends with a column after "Wskazania" that shows the hours of the schedule against the weekly norm of the person's FTE, on two lines: the sum with the norm ("32 h z 40 h") and the difference in words ("mniej o 8 h", "więcej o 2 h 30 min" or "równo z normą"; a sum of zero reads "0 min"). It is counted again in the island after every click, from the same optimistic `list` of `useAssignments` as the violations (so a click taken back after a failed save takes its hours back too; no request, nothing stored, no migration). The rules are pure functions in `src/lib/services/workload.ts` (`summarizeWorkload` makes one `WorkloadRow` for each person, `formatWorkload` the text) on top of `work-hours.ts`; the hook is `src/components/hooks/useWorkload.ts` (one `useMemo` over `list`, a row for each person of the roster of the schedule, the dictionary shifts only like `useViolations`), and the cell is a `td` of its own in `AssignmentGrid.tsx`, never part of the `th scope="row"` or of the `aria-label` of a shift button (the smoke test compares those exactly). A paragraph under the people's table says what the column is made of.
- The definition of the weekly norm in that column (the same as the `weekly_norm` warning): 40 h times the FTE, less 8 h times the FTE for each holiday on a day other than Sunday among the seven days of the schedule (and, since S-07, less the approved absences of the person, see the absences paragraphs). The sum is that of the full durations of the shifts that start in the schedule (a night shift on the last day counts in full). The norm is rounded to the minute to be shown, and the difference is taken from the rounded value, while the live rule `weekly_norm` compares exactly: with 3/7 of an FTE in a week without a holiday the norm is 1028.57 minutes, shown as 17 h 9 min, so 1029 minutes reads "równo z normą" although the rule warns about it. The column has no threshold, no status and no colour as a carrier (exceeding the norm is the warning of the list), and, since S-07, the approved absences of a person lower the norm for them (decision D7, an interpretation of art. 130 § 3; see the absences paragraphs below), and the paragraph under the table says so.
- Absences (S-07: an employee away on whole days, entered by the planner; planner-only like the rest of the roster data). The table `absences` (migration `supabase/migrations/20261126204500_absences.sql`) has `id`, `employee_id` (`on delete cascade`, indexed), `type` (`vacation`, `sick_leave` or `other`), `status` (`requested`, `approved` or `rejected`, default `approved`), `start_date` and `end_date` (the first and the last day, both included) and `created_at`, and four CHECK constraints: `absences_type_valid`, `absences_status_valid`, `absences_dates_valid` (both days from 2020-01-01 to 2099-12-31) and `absences_range_valid` (the end not before the start). The date bounds are load-bearing: they are those of `schedules.start_date` and of `parseIsoDate`, and a date outside them would make the date helpers of the rules throw a `RangeError`, which `safeEvaluate` answers with "not checked" for every schedule that person is on, so the database refuses such a date instead of leaving the app to cope. There is no text column and no hours on purpose (an L4 is health data, so the table holds the type and the days and nothing else), and nothing stops the absences of one person from overlapping (an L4 during a leave is legal; the rules take the strongest). RLS is planner-only with one policy for each operation (an employee has no policy until S-13 and S-11 add an own-row one; the cascade from `employees` only runs when the service-role key removes an employee, as nobody deletes employees through the API). The column grants: a session inserts `employee_id`, `type`, `start_date` and `end_date` and updates `type`, `start_date` and `end_date`, so `status` is never written by a session (the default `approved` is what every entry of the planner gets: an entry by the planner is approved by itself) and the person of an absence does not change (moving it is removing it and adding another); a write that names another column is refused with 42501. Rows with the status `requested` or `rejected` come from the service-role key (the smoke test writes them) until S-14 (leave requests) and S-15 (their decision) add the writes they need in migrations of their own, the way S-09 will for `schedule_assignments.source`.
- The absence modules, routes and pages. The pure rules are in `src/lib/services/absence.ts` (no `astro:*`; dates only through `parseIsoDate` and UTC arithmetic, never `Date.parse`, which ESLint forbids there): `validateAbsenceInput` (one function for the form and the routes: a UUID, a type of `ABSENCE_TYPES`, two dates in 2020-2099, the end not before the start, one Polish message for each field; other keys such as `status` or `id` are left out of the value, not passed on), `absenceInterval`, `dayOffsetOf` and `rangesOverlap` (the timeline), `excusedWeekdays` (the norm), `absenceMarkers` and `formatAbsenceMarker` (the cells), `formatAbsenceRange` with `absenceDayCount` and `formatAbsenceDays` (the number of calendar days, both ends included, that the list writes after the range: "(3 dni)", "(1 dzień)"), `sortAbsences` (latest start first, then `id`), `absencesOfRoster` (keeps the absences of the people of a schedule: the filter that `listAbsencesOverlapping` leaves to its caller) and `absenceStatusLabel`, with `lowerFirst` and `formatDayAndMonth` shared with `rules.ts`. What a status means for the rules is decided once, in an exhaustive `Record<AbsenceStatus, ...>` of `absence.ts` (`isAbsenceJudged`, `isAbsenceConfirmed`, `absenceStatusRank`), which `rules.ts`, `absenceMarkers`, `formatAbsenceMarker` and `excusedWeekdays` all use, so a status added to `AbsenceStatus` does not compile until somebody decides what it does. The data module is `src/lib/absences.ts`, the only one that knows the table and its columns, always on the session of the user: `listAbsencesPage` (the list of the page: sorted and cut by the database, at most `ABSENCE_LIST_LIMIT` (500) rows and one more read to tell whether there are more, optionally only the absences that end on or after a day), `listAbsencesOverlapping(supabase, from, to)` (the absences of everybody that share a day with `from` to `to`; the caller keeps the people it needs with `absencesOfRoster`), `getAbsence`, `createAbsence`, `updateAbsence` (the type and the two days only: an `employeeId` in the input is ignored) and `deleteAbsence`; the read that feeds the rules (`listAbsencesOverlapping`) asks the database for an exact count and refuses an answer shorter than it (`assertCompleteRead`, so a cut-off answer is caught whatever `max_rows` the hosted project has), and it still throws when the answer reaches `READ_LIMIT` (1000 rows, the local `max_rows` of PostgREST) as the second guard, because the rules would judge an incomplete set; `assignments.ts` does the same since S-08. `toAbsenceOptions` (`src/lib/schedule-options.ts`) makes the `AbsenceOption`s the island gets (`src/types.ts`: `employeeId`, `type`, `status`, `startDate`, `endDate`, never the id of the row, its creation time or an e-mail address). The routes `POST /api/absences` (201 `{ absence }`), `PUT /api/absences/[id]` (200) and `DELETE /api/absences/[id]` (200 `{ ok: true }`, idempotent like the removal of an assignment: only an id that is not a UUID gets 404) answer 415 or 400 for a bad body, 503 `not_configured`, 404 `not_found` (`PUT`), 422 with `errors` for the fields or `unknown_reference` for a person who does not exist, and 500 `failed`, all with `no-store`. The pages are planner-only, inside `PlannerLayout` and `no-store` (they carry names and kinds of absences): `/absences` (a table from `md` up and cards below it, latest start first, the person as "Last name First name" from `toEmployeeOptions` and never an e-mail address, the days with their number after the range, the status in words: "Zatwierdzona", "Wnioskowana", "Odrzucona"; two views, set by the query parameter `all`: by default the absences that ended in the last twelve months or still go on (`end_date` on or after today in UTC less 365 days, worked out in the page) and, only for `?all=1`, every absence, each at most 500 rows and with a note that names the view and a link to the other one, a note of its own when the list is cut, and a heading that counts the rows of the view), `/absences/new` and `/absences/[id]` (the `src/components/absences/AbsenceForm.tsx` island with its `useAbsenceForm` hook: native selects for the person and the type, with a hint under the type that says an "Inna nieobecność" counts as excused and lowers the norm like a leave (`aria-describedby`), two `type="date"` fields with the range as a visible hint; when editing, the person is read-only, the status is shown as read-only words ("Status: Zatwierdzona", `absenceStatusLabel`) between the heading and the form, and an unknown id gets 404 with a block of its own), `src/components/absences/DeleteAbsenceButton.tsx` with `useDeleteAbsence` (removal after a two-step confirmation) and the card "Nieobecności" of `/planner`. `access.ts` did not change (default deny already makes the paths planner-only) and `access.test.ts` has their cases.
- Absences in the live check. `RuleInput.absences` is optional (`AbsenceOption[]`), so an input without it is judged as before, and `useViolations` passes the prop `absences` of `AssignmentGrid` to `safeEvaluate`. Two rules joined `ViolationRule`: `absence` (critical: an assignment overlaps an approved absence of its person) and `absence_requested` (a warning: it overlaps only a requested one); a rejected absence is ignored. They are two rules and not one with a weight that varies, because S-08 confirms warnings by key and S-15 will turn a request into an approved absence: under one key a warning would silently become critical. One violation for each assignment, with the weight and the reason of the strongest absence it overlaps (an approved one before a requested one, then the one that starts earlier, then the longer, then by type); the key is `absence|<employee id>|<anchor of the assignment>` (or `absence_requested|...`), the anchor is the assignment alone, `actual` is the minutes of the shift inside the absence, `limit` is 0 and `unit` is `minutes`; an assignment to a shift outside the dictionary has no hours and is left out. `RULE_RANK` in `rules.ts` is `daily_rest` 0, `daily_hours` 1, `weekly_hours` 2, `weekly_rest` 3, `absence` 4, `weekly_norm` 5, `understaffed` 6 and `absence_requested` 7, so `absence` is the last of the critical ones and `absence_requested` the last of the warnings (changing a rank is safe, as no key or stored data holds it, and a rule with no rank does not compile). The collision is judged on the minutes timeline of the rules: an absence covers whole days, from midnight of its first day to the end of its last (`absenceInterval`, a half-open interval, `rangesOverlap`), so a night shift that starts the evening before an absence and runs into its first day collides with it, and a shift that ends exactly at midnight when the absence starts does not. A night shift that starts on the last day of the schedule runs into the day after it, so the assignment page reads the absences of the seven days and the day after them (`listAbsencesOverlapping(supabase, startDate, addDays(startDate, PERIOD_DAYS))`, then `absencesOfRoster` on `schedule.employeeIds`, then `toAbsenceOptions`). The coverage ("x z y") is unchanged: a person on leave still counts as assigned, and the collision is reported once, as the critical item.
- The correction of the weekly norm (decision D7 of `context/foundation/work-time-rules.md`: an interpretation of art. 130, accepted by the author on 2026-11-26, not legal advice): an approved absence lowers the norm by 8 h times the FTE for each distinct day from Monday to Friday, not a holiday, among the seven days of the schedule that it covers, never below 0 (then any work is above the norm). `excusedWeekdays` finds those days (several absences on one day count once, a Saturday or a Sunday lowers nothing, a day the holiday has already lowered is not lowered again, a requested or a rejected absence lowers nothing) and `weeklyNormExcused(holidays, excusedDays, fte)` in `work-hours.ts` does the arithmetic (`weeklyNorm`, `weeklyNormWithHolidays` and the shape of `WeeklyNorm` are unchanged, so `normMinutes` and `exceedsNorm` take its result as it is). The same helper feeds the `weekly_norm` warning, whose reason then ends "po pomniejszeniu o święto 11.11 i nieobecność (2 dni: 12.11, 13.11)", and the column "Godziny" (`summarizeWorkload`, `WorkloadInput.absences`, `useWorkload`), so the two never show two norms for one week; the paragraph under the table (`HOURS_NOTE` in `AssignmentGrid.tsx`, the same words are in the smoke test) says so. The absences are what the page loaded and nothing in the grid changes them, so the live check still requests and stores nothing (no migration for it) and they only join the dependencies of the `useMemo` of the two hooks. In the cell of a person and a day, under the shift buttons, `absenceMarkers` and `formatAbsenceMarker` write "Nieobecność: urlop" (or "zwolnienie lekarskie (L4)", "inna nieobecność") and, for a requested absence, "(niezatwierdzona)" after the type, so that the words carry the difference and not a colour; a rejected absence has no marker, and when several absences cover a day the strongest one is named, and for a shift within one day it is the one the reason of a violation names (a night shift that runs into the next day can differ: the marker names the strongest absence that covers the day of the cell, the reason the strongest one that the interval of the shift overlaps, e.g. a requested absence on the day and an approved one from the next day; `absence.test.ts` pins that difference). The marker is information and not a violation (it never says "Krytyczne" or "Ostrzeżenie", it is no `role="alert"`, its icon is `aria-hidden`) and it is text in the existing cell: no new column, no new table, nothing in the `th scope="row"` or in the `aria-label` of a shift button, because the smoke test compares those and indexes the cells by position.

- Schedule approval (S-08). A schedule is a draft until the planner approves it on `/schedules/<id>/assign`; approval is final (nothing takes it back, and a change of an approved schedule will be a new version, S-12). The lock is in the database (migration `*_schedule_approval.sql`): `schedules.status` (`draft` or `approved`) is written by no session, because the table-wide grant was replaced by column grants on `start_date` and `label`; the write policies of `schedules`, `schedule_staffing`, `schedule_employees` and `schedule_assignments` refuse an approved schedule through `public.schedule_is_draft(uuid)` (true also for an id with no schedule, so such a write is still refused by the foreign key); and `save_schedule` raises `PT423` for an approved one (it checks after its update, so a save that waited for an approval is told "approved" and not "not found"). The cascades and the service-role key bypass RLS, which the smoke cleanup relies on.
- The one door is the SQL function `public.approve_schedule` (`security definer`, `set search_path = ''`, execute for signed-in users, the role check is its first statement). It locks the schedule row, then `schedule_staffing`, `schedule_employees`, `schedule_assignments` and `absences` against writes (staffing first: every save writes it first, so the other order could deadlock), locks the used shifts and the people of the roster, compares the data the app judged (`p_inputs`) set by set with the live rows, refuses findings that are not warnings and warnings that were not confirmed, and stores the record `schedule_approvals` (time, `approved_by` and e-mail without a key to `auth.users`, the findings, the confirmed keys, the counts) with the snapshots `schedule_approval_shifts` and `schedule_approval_employees` (name, hours, FTE, worked and norm minutes). Its errors: `42501` not a planner, `PT404`, `PT423` approved already, `PT412` data changed, `PT422` findings or hours not acceptable. The three tables are readable by a planner only and written by nobody else; an employee gets no policy, because the frozen reasons name the kind of an absence (sick leave included): S-11 must not give employees access to `schedule_approvals`.
- The route `POST /api/schedules/[id]/approve` (`{ confirmedKeys, basis, assignments }`) reads the data with the planner's session, answers 409 `schedule_changed` when what the page shows is not what the database holds (`basis` is the SHA-256 of `approvalBasis` over the data the grid does not change, `assignments` the grid's list; the page puts the former on the approval section as `data-approval-basis`), counts the rules on the server (`safeEvaluate`, the same reads and the same pure functions as the assignment page, so the texts are the ones the planner saw), decides with `decideApproval` and calls the function through `src/lib/approvals.ts`. It answers 200 `{ approvedAt }`, 404, 409 `critical_violations` / `confirmation_required` (with the findings) / `schedule_changed` / `schedule_approved`, 422 `invalid_body`, 503 `rules_unavailable` / `not_configured` and 500 `failed`; `PUT /api/schedules/[id]` and the assignment routes answer 409 `schedule_approved` for an approved schedule. The pure parts are in `src/lib/services/approval.ts` (the decision, the payload, the messages, the Polish day of an approval).
- Accepted risks of the approval. A planner who calls the function directly can hand it a shortened list of findings (the database does not repeat the rules); the record keeps who approved. The confirmation compares keys only (the author's decision of 2026-11-27): a warning that changed its numbers under the same key between the confirmation panel and the approval (a shortage of 1 that grew to 3) passes as confirmed, while the record stores the findings counted on the server.
- An approved schedule is read only on every page and is shown from its approval, never from the live data: the assignment page shows the banner "Grafik zatwierdzony dd.mm.rrrr, przez <e-mail>", disabled shift buttons, the stored findings and hours (nothing is counted again; `useViolations` and `useWorkload` take them as `stored`) and the shifts and people of the snapshot (`snapshotShiftOptions`, `snapshotEmployeeOptions`, `snapshotHours` in `src/lib/schedule-options.ts`); `/schedules/<id>` shows a message instead of the form; the list has a column "Status" and only the link "Podgląd" for an approved one; the D6 notice has a variant for it. So editing a shift or a person, or entering an absence later, changes nothing on an approved schedule. The approval section of a draft (`src/components/schedules/ApprovalSection.tsx` with `useApproval`) comes after the coverage table, is disabled with its reason while there is a critical violation, and always asks for a confirmation.
- The smoke test covers the approval too: the lock and the function through PostgREST as planner, employee, role-less account and anonymous caller, the route on a schedule of its own (`Smoke-<runId>-approve-route`) and the pages of a draft and of the approved schedule, also after its shift and person are edited. The concurrency of the locks was checked once with two connections (`context/changes/schedule-approval/reviews/lock-test.md`, moved with the change when it is archived).

### Key conventions

- **Path alias**: `@/*` maps to `./src/*` (tsconfig paths).
- **Astro components** for static content/layout; **React components** only when interactivity is needed.
- **Tailwind class merging**: use the `cn()` helper from `@/lib/utils` (clsx + tailwind-merge) for conditional/merged class names. Do not concatenate class strings manually.
- **shadcn/ui**: components live in `src/components/ui/`, "new-york" style variant. Install new ones with `npx shadcn@latest add [name]`.
- **API routes**: use uppercase `GET`, `POST` exports; validate input with zod.
- **Supabase migrations**: `supabase/migrations/` using naming format `YYYYMMDDHHmmss_short_description.sql`. Always enable RLS on new tables with granular per-operation, per-role policies. The role helpers `public.current_app_role()` / `public.is_planner()` (they read `app_metadata.role` from the JWT, `null` / `false` for anything unknown) live in the first migration (`supabase/migrations/*_role_helpers.sql`), whose trailing comment holds the RLS policy pattern to copy.
- **React**: no Next.js directives ("use client" etc.). Extract hooks to `src/components/hooks/`.
- **Services/helpers** go in `src/lib/` (or `src/lib/services/` for extracted business logic).
- **Shared types** (entities, DTOs) go in `src/types.ts`.

## 10xDevs AI Toolkit — Module 1, Lesson 1

Bootstrap a greenfield project end-to-end with the **shaping chain**:

```
/10x-init  →  /10x-shape  →  /10x-prd  →  (10x-tech-stack-selector)  →  (bootstrapper)
```

The first three skills ship in this lesson; the last two are the next links in the chain.

### Task Router — Where to start

| Skill | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Project setup** | |
| `/10x-init` | The project directory is fresh. Scaffolds `context/foundation/lessons.md` and `docs/reference/contract-surfaces.md` so the rest of the workflow has somewhere to write. Run this once per project. |
| **Discovery** | |
| `/10x-shape` | You have an idea and need to turn it into structured shape-notes BEFORE writing a PRD. Greenfield only. Walks vision → persona/access → MVP → FRs (with Socratic challenge) → business logic & data → stack-openness sketch. Surfaces empty-CRUD and MVP-too-big anti-patterns by name. Output: `context/foundation/shape-notes.md` with a resumable `checkpoint:` block. |
| **Document generation** | |
| `/10x-prd` | You have shape-notes (or raw notes) and want a schema-conformant `context/foundation/prd.md`. Generates against the locked schema, routes every gap verbatim into `## Open Questions`, and refuses to invent domain decisions. On collision, prompts overwrite vs. versioned save (`prd-vN.md`). |

### How the chain hands off

- `/10x-init` produces the workflow v2 scaffold (`context/foundation/`, `lessons.md`, `contract-surfaces.md`). `/10x-shape` requires this and will offer to delegate to `/10x-init` if it's missing.
- `/10x-shape` writes `context/foundation/shape-notes.md` with frontmatter `checkpoint:` (current_phase, phases_completed, frs_drafted, quality_check_status). On re-entry, it resumes from the next unfinished phase.
- `/10x-prd` reads `shape-notes.md` (default) or any path you pass, scores the input on a 4-signal heuristic, warns on thin input, and writes `context/foundation/prd.md` against the schema at `skills/10x-shape/references/prd-schema.md` (frontmatter aligned 1:1 with 10x-tech-stack-selector's Q1–Q7).

### What the PRD captures (and what it does NOT)

- **Captured**: vision, persona, success criteria, user stories (Given/When/Then), FRs (FR-NNN), NFRs, business logic (one-sentence rule first), data model, access control, durable implementation decisions, testing strategy, deployment & CI/CD strategy, non-goals, open questions.
- **NOT captured (deliberate)**: framework choices, database choices, file paths, deployment platform. Stack openness is binding — only `product_type` and `tech_preferences.language_family` capture stack-shaped intent. Frameworks are 10x-tech-stack-selector's job.

### Anti-patterns surfaced during shaping

- **Empty-CRUD**: business logic that reduces to "users add and remove records" with no domain rule. `/10x-shape` names it explicitly and prompts for a real rule shape (recommendation, prioritization, classification, validation, scoring, workflow, calculation).
- **MVP-too-big**: first-flow estimate exceeds ~1 week of after-hours work, or > 4 distinct user actions before user-visible value, or requires multiple integrations before payoff. Skill names the expensive pieces and offers concrete scope-down moves.

Both are **soft gates**: they warn but allow override. Overrides are recorded in the checkpoint and surfaced in the PRD's `## Open Questions`.

### Foundation paths used by this lesson

- `context/foundation/shape-notes.md` — `/10x-shape` output
- `context/foundation/prd.md` (or `prd-vN.md`) — `/10x-prd` output
- `context/foundation/lessons.md` — recurring rules & pitfalls (scaffolded by `/10x-init`)
- `docs/reference/contract-surfaces.md` — load-bearing names registry (scaffolded by `/10x-init`)

### Universal language

The shipped skills carry no 10xDevs / cohort / certification references. The mechanics (Socratic challenge, gray-area discovery, recommended-answer fatigue mitigation, soft quality gate) are universal indicators of a well-scoped greenfield project.

Skills must not write to `context/archive/`. Archived changes are immutable; if a resolved target path starts with `context/archive/`, abort with: "This change is archived. Open a new change with `/10x-new` instead."
